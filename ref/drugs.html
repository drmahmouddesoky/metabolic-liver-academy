Drug treatment for MASH

Two drugs are approved for non-cirrhotic MASH with F2–F3 fibrosis: resmetirom and semaglutide.

For healthcare professionals

Key points

  • Who: adults with MASH and moderate-to-advanced fibrosis (F2–F3), no cirrhosis, alongside lifestyle change.
  • Biopsy is not required. Non-invasive tests can identify likely F2–F3.
  • Approvals differ by country. Check local status (in Saudi Arabia, the SFDA) before prescribing.
  • Neither is approved for cirrhosis yet.

Approved in the US (resmetirom 2024, semaglutide 2025), the EU (resmetirom, conditional, 2025) and the UK (both, 2026). Long-term outcome trials are ongoing.

Choosing between them

  • Obesity or type 2 diabetes needing treatment anyway: semaglutide treats both.
  • Normal weight, GLP-1 not tolerated, or already on a GLP-1 drug: resmetirom is an option.
  • Data on using both together are still limited.

Monitoring response

There is no single agreed stopping rule yet. Reassess about once a year with weight, ALT/AST and a non-invasive test (LSM, ELF or FIB-4). A clear fall in ALT and liver stiffness suggests benefit. The 2026 global consensus gives a framework for this.
