Drug treatment for MASH

Two drugs are approved for non-cirrhotic MASH with F2–F3 fibrosis: resmetirom and semaglutide.

For
Healthcare professionals
Author
Dr. Mahmoud Al-Desoky
Version
1.0, updated 2026-10-03

Contents

  1. The two approved drugs
  2. Who qualifies for resmetirom (AASLD 2024)
  3. Choosing between them

  5. Response at 12 months
  6. Other medicines
  7. References

Key points

  • Who: adults with MASH and moderate-to-advanced fibrosis (F2–F3), no cirrhosis, alongside lifestyle change.
  • Biopsy is not required. Non-invasive tests can identify likely F2–F3.
  • Approvals differ by country. The Saudi 2026 consensus noted that resmetirom was not yet approved in Saudi Arabia; check current SFDA status before prescribing.
  • Neither is approved for cirrhosis yet.

For the evidence, graded recommendations, a case and self-test questions, read Module 2: Choosing drug treatment for MASH.

2Who qualifies for resmetirom (AASLD 2024)

RecommendedNot recommended
MASLD with VCTE 8–15 kPa, or MRE 3.1–4.4 kPa, or biopsy-proven MASH F2–F3Cirrhosis, including VCTE >20 kPa or MRE >5 kPa
Values outside these ranges: individual decision by a liver specialistOther active liver disease; alcohol >20 g/day (women) or >30 g/day (men); active thyroid disease

The 2025 global consensus also allows treatment at 16–20 kPa if a second test excludes cirrhosis and there are no signs of portal hypertension.

3Choosing between them

  • Obesity or type 2 diabetes needing treatment anyway: semaglutide treats both.
  • Normal weight, GLP-1 not tolerated, or already on a GLP-1 drug: resmetirom is an option.
  • Data on using both together are still limited.

4Monitoring resmetirom

  • Safety: liver panel at 3, 6 and 12 months.
  • Stop for liver injury: AST or ALT >5× the upper limit, or bilirubin >2.5 mg/dL with raised enzymes, among other label criteria.
  • Response at 12 months, using the same test as at baseline (image-based stiffness preferred).

5Response at 12 months

FindingAction
LSM falls ≥25% on VCTE (≥20% on MRE), or ALT normalises or clearly improvesBenefit: continue
Small fall in LSM, no clear ALT changeUncertain: re-optimise lifestyle, consider other options
Non-invasive tests worsen, or ALT keeps risingNo response: stop

Based on the AASLD October 2024 update. The 2025 global consensus uses a ≥30% change in VCTE stiffness, and says ALT alone should not decide response.

6Other medicines

DrugPlace in MASLD
PioglitazoneImproved histology in trials. AASLD: can be considered with type 2 diabetes. EASL and the global consensus: safe, but use it for diabetes, not as a MASH treatment. Weight gain and fluid retention.
Vitamin EAASLD: can be considered in selected people. Global consensus: only in selected people without diabetes or cirrhosis. EASL 2024: not recommended as MASH treatment.
Tirzepatide, other GLP-1 drugsUse for diabetes or obesity. Liver data promising, not yet approved for MASH.
SGLT2 inhibitorsUse for diabetes, heart and kidney benefit. Not a liver treatment.
MetforminSafe; no effect on histology.
StatinsSafe and recommended for heart risk, including compensated cirrhosis.
Ursodeoxycholic acid, silymarin, other supplementsNot recommended.
