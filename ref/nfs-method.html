NFS calculator: method, users and limits

How our NAFLD fibrosis score calculator works, and when not to rely on it.

Key points

  • For healthcare professionals assessing adults with known or suspected MASLD.
  • Estimates the chance of advanced fibrosis (F3–F4). Accuracy is similar to FIB-4 in people over 35.
  • Obesity and diabetes raise the score, so false positives are more common in these groups.
  • Runs only in the browser. Nothing typed is stored or sent.

For the evidence, graded recommendations, a case and self-test questions, read Module 1: Assessing liver fibrosis in MASLD.

1Formula and units

NFS = −1.675 + 0.037 × age + 0.094 × BMI + 1.13 × (impaired fasting glucose or diabetes: 1, else 0) + 0.99 × AST/ALT − 0.013 × platelets (10⁹/L) − 0.66 × albumin (g/dL).

The calculator works out BMI from weight and height, and converts albumin from g/L to g/dL (divide by 10) if you choose g/L.

Worked example: age 55, weight 90 kg, height 170 cm (BMI 31.1), diabetes yes, AST 40, ALT 35, platelets 200, albumin 4.2 g/dL → 0.18 → indeterminate.

2How results are labelled

ScoreLabel
Below −1.455 (below 0.12 if aged 65+)Low risk
From the low cut-off up to 0.676Indeterminate
Above 0.676High risk

Cut-offs from Angulo 2007; the age-65 cut-off from McPherson 2017.

3Do not rely on NFS alone when

  • The patient is under 35 (accuracy is poor).
  • BMI is 35 or more, or the patient has diabetes: the score runs high. Confirm with a second-line test.
  • Albumin is low for another reason (e.g. kidney or bowel protein loss, malnutrition, acute illness).
  • Platelets are low for another reason, or there is acute liver injury.
  • Children and pregnancy: not validated.

4Why there is no APRI calculator

APRI was built for hepatitis C. In MASLD it is less accurate than FIB-4 and NFS, and the EASL and AASLD guidance prefer FIB-4 as the first step.

5How we check the calculator

The code is public on GitHub. Before each change we test known values, including the worked example, both age groups and both albumin units.
