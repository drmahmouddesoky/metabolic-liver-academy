The two-step fibrosis pathway

FIB-4 first, then elastography or ELF if FIB-4 is not low.

For healthcare professionals

Key points

  • Step 1: FIB-4. Below 1.3 (below 2.0 if aged 65+) = low risk.
  • Step 2: liver stiffness (VCTE) or ELF if FIB-4 is 1.3 or more.
  • LSM below 8 kPa = low risk. 8–12 = indeterminate. 12 or more = high risk: refer to hepatology.
  • FIB-4 is not reliable under age 35.

Step 1: FIB-4

FIB-4 = (age × AST) ÷ (platelets [10⁹/L] × √ALT).

FIB-4MeaningAction
<1.3 (<2.0 if ≥65 y)Low risk of advanced fibrosisManage risk factors in primary care. Repeat in 1–3 years.
1.3–2.67IndeterminateSecond test: VCTE or ELF.
≥2.67High riskSecond test, and usually refer.

Step 2: liver stiffness or ELF

ResultMeaningAction
LSM <8 kPaLow riskPrimary care follow-up
LSM 8–12 kPaIndeterminateSpecialist review, or add ELF / MRE
LSM ≥12 kPaAdvanced fibrosis likelyRefer to hepatology
LSM ≥15 kPaCompensated advanced chronic liver disease likelyHepatology; assess portal hypertension
ELF <7.7Low riskPrimary care follow-up
ELF ≥9.8High riskRefer to hepatology

Cut-offs follow the EASL–EASD–EASO 2024 and AASLD 2023 guidance. Some services use other ELF cut-offs, so check your lab.

Pitfalls

  • Age under 35: FIB-4 and NFS perform poorly. Go straight to VCTE if you are worried.
  • Age 65+: use the 2.0 lower cut-off to cut false positives.
  • Acute hepatitis, very high ALT, or low platelets from another cause can mislead FIB-4.
  • A low score in a patient with clinical signs of cirrhosis does not exclude it.
