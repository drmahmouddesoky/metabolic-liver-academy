Doctors' reference › Follow up and special groups

Special groups

Children, lean patients, older adults, young adults and people who drink alcohol.

For healthcare professionals

Key points

  • Children: screen with ALT from age 9–11 in obesity. FIB-4 is not valid.
  • Lean MASLD (BMI <25, <23 Asian) can still progress. Use the same pathway.
  • Age 65+: FIB-4 lower cut-off 2.0; NFS 0.12.
  • MetALD: treat both the alcohol use and the metabolic risk.

Children and adolescents

  • Screen with ALT from age 9–11 in children with obesity, or overweight with extra risk factors.
  • Interpret ALT with child ranges (about 22 U/L girls, 26 U/L boys). More than twice that persistently needs work-up.
  • Look harder for other causes (Wilson disease, autoimmune hepatitis, genetic disorders).
  • Family-based lifestyle change is the main treatment. No drug is approved for MASH in children.

Lean MASLD

Check waist size, diet, activity and genetics (e.g. PNPLA3). Rule out other causes carefully. A 3–5% weight loss can help.

Under 35 and over 65

Blood scores perform poorly under 35; use elastography if concerned. At 65 or over, use the age-adjusted cut-offs to avoid over-referral.

MetALD

Ask about alcohol in grams per week. Use a validated tool (AUDIT-C) and offer support for alcohol use. Fibrosis progresses faster when both causes are present.
