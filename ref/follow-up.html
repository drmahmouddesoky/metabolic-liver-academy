Follow-up and referral

How often to retest, and when to send the patient to a liver specialist.

Key points

  • Low risk: repeat FIB-4 every 1–2 years with diabetes or several risk factors, every 2–3 years otherwise.
  • Indeterminate: repeat second-line test in about a year, or refer.
  • High risk (LSM ≥12 kPa, ELF ≥9.8): hepatology.
  • Any sign of cirrhosis or decompensation: urgent referral.

For the evidence, graded recommendations, a case and self-test questions, read Module 1: Assessing liver fibrosis in MASLD.

1Refer to hepatology if

  • LSM ≥12 kPa or ELF ≥9.8, or FIB-4 ≥2.67 without access to a second test.
  • Clinical, lab or imaging signs of cirrhosis.
  • Doubt about the diagnosis or another liver disease suspected.
  • Candidate for MASH drug treatment.
  • ALT persistently above 3 times normal.

2Red flags: same-day care

  • Jaundice, confusion, vomiting blood or black stools, rapid abdominal swelling.

3Every visit

  • Weight and waist, blood pressure.
  • Diabetes and lipid control.
  • Alcohol and smoking.
  • Review new medicines for liver effects.
