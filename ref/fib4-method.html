FIB-4 calculator: method, users and limits

How our FIB-4 calculator works, who it is for, and when not to rely on it.

For
Healthcare professionals
Author
Dr. Mahmoud Desoky
Version
1.0, updated 2026-10-03

  1. Formula and units
  2. How results are labelled
  3. Do not rely on FIB-4 alone when

  5. References

Key points

  • For healthcare professionals assessing adults with known or suspected MASLD.
  • It estimates the chance of advanced fibrosis (F3–F4). It does not diagnose MASLD or MASH.
  • It is a first step. Results of 1.3 or more need a second test.
  • Runs only in the browser. Nothing typed is stored or sent.

For the evidence, graded recommendations, a case and self-test questions, read Module 1: Assessing liver fibrosis in MASLD.

1Formula and units

FIB-4 = (age in years × AST in U/L) ÷ (platelets in 10⁹/L × √ALT in U/L).

Platelets in 10⁹/L are the same number as in 10³/µL. Use AST and ALT taken on the same day.

InputAccepted range
Age18–100 years
ASTAbove 0 and below 5000 U/L
ALTAbove 0 and below 5000 U/L
PlateletsAbove 0 and below 2000 ×10⁹/L

Worked example: age 55, AST 40, ALT 35, platelets 200 → (55 × 40) ÷ (200 × 5.92) = 1.86 → indeterminate.

2How results are labelled

ScoreLabel
Below 1.3 (below 2.0 if aged 65+)Low risk
From the low cut-off up to below 2.67Indeterminate
2.67 or aboveHigh risk

Cut-offs from the EASL–EASD–EASO 2024 and AASLD 2023 guidance; the age-65 cut-off from McPherson 2017.

3Do not rely on FIB-4 alone when

  • The patient is under 35 (accuracy is poor). The calculator shows a warning.
  • AST or ALT is very high, or there is acute liver injury. The calculator warns above 200 U/L.
  • Platelets are low for another reason (e.g. blood disease, hypersplenism from another cause, recent chemotherapy).
  • There are clinical or imaging signs of cirrhosis. A low score does not exclude it.
  • Another liver disease is present. FIB-4 cut-offs here are for MASLD.
  • Children and pregnancy: not validated.

4How we check the calculator

The code is public on GitHub. Before each change we test known values, including the worked example above and the cut-off edges (1.29, 1.30, 2.66, 2.67), and both age groups. Report a problem by email.
