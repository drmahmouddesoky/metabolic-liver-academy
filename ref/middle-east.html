Fatty liver in Saudi Arabia and the Middle East

The numbers behind our mission: how common MASLD is in our region, and the gaps we need to close.

For
Healthcare professionals
Author
Dr. Mahmoud Al Desoky
Version
1.0, updated 2026-10-03
Peer review

Key points

  • About 39% of people in the Middle East and North Africa have MASLD, and about 69% of people with type 2 diabetes.
  • In Saudi Arabia, estimated adult prevalence rose from 41% (2012) to 44% (2019).
  • Models predict cirrhosis and liver cancer from MASH in Saudi Arabia will roughly triple from 2018 to 2030.
  • Doctors' knowledge varies, and the region produces only about 3% of world MASLD research.

Modelled and pooled estimates. Local studies vary with the test used and the group studied.

2Where it is heading

  • A Saudi model (2018–2030) predicts people with F0–F3 fibrosis doubling to 2.5 million, and cirrhosis plus liver cancer cases tripling to about 212,000.
  • The same model estimated NASH-related costs at about 5.8% of national health spending in 2019.
  • In Saudi Arabia, MASLD is rising while hepatitis B is falling and hepatitis C is stable. Fatty liver is becoming the main chronic liver disease.
  • Across the region, high fasting blood glucose is the largest risk factor driving the MASLD burden.

3What patients tell us

In a survey of 804 Saudi patients, 79% called their condition "fatty liver" frequently or always, and only 17% had told family or friends about it. Few reported stigma, but 43% had missed or avoided a primary care visit because of their liver disease.

This is why our patient pages say "fatty liver" (الكبد الدهني) first and explain MASLD second.

4The gaps

  • Knowledge: in a survey of 584 doctors from Saudi Arabia, Egypt and Türkiye, guideline adherence was 81–84% among specialists but 38–51% among non-specialists.
  • Research: from 2014 to 2023, Arab countries produced 844 MASLD papers, about 3.3% of the world total, despite some of the highest prevalence.
