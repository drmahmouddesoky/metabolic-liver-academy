The fibrosis pathway in four steps

For adults with MASLD or at risk of it. Summarised from the EASL–EASD–EASO 2024 clinical practice guideline.

  1. Find people at risk

    Type 2 diabetes, abdominal obesity with at least one other metabolic risk factor, or persistently raised liver enzymes.

  2. Calculate FIB-4

    Below 1.3: low risk; manage metabolic risk and re-check in 1 to 3 years. 1.3 or above: go to step 3. Open the calculator.

  3. Measure liver stiffness

    Transient elastography (VCTE), or ELF if not available. Below 8 kPa: low risk, stay in primary care. 8 to 12 kPa: indeterminate. 12 kPa or above: advanced fibrosis likely.

  4. Refer to a liver specialist

    Indeterminate or high results go to hepatology for full assessment, treatment decisions and, when needed, cancer surveillance.

Source: EASL–EASD–EASO Clinical Practice Guidelines on the management of MASLD. J Hepatol 2024;81:492–542.

Lifestyle: the base of every treatment plan

Every patient with MASLD needs a lifestyle plan, with or without medicines.

Weight loss What it does to the liver
≥ 5%Less liver fat
7–10%Less inflammation; MASH can resolve
≥ 10%Fibrosis can improve

Food

  • A Mediterranean-style pattern: vegetables, legumes, whole grains, fish, olive oil, nuts.
  • Cut sugary drinks and juices, sweets, white bread and white rice.
  • Limit ultra-processed foods and fried food.
  • Gulf tip: swap kabsa white rice for a smaller portion with salad first; dates in small numbers.

Activity, alcohol and smoking

  • At least 150 minutes a week of moderate activity (for example brisk walking), or 75 minutes of vigorous activity.
  • Add resistance exercise twice a week. Exercise helps the liver even without weight loss.
  • Reduce alcohol; stop completely if there is advanced fibrosis or cirrhosis.
  • Stop smoking: it adds to heart and liver cancer risk.

Drugs

Two medicines are now approved for MASH with moderate-to-advanced fibrosis (F2–F3) without cirrhosis. Both are add-ons to lifestyle change, not replacements. Approval in Saudi Arabia and other Gulf countries should be checked locally.

Medicine How it works Approvals for MASH
Resmetirom Oral, once daily. Activates the liver's thyroid hormone receptor-β to burn liver fat. US FDA, March 2024 (accelerated) · European Commission, August 2025 (conditional) · UK MHRA, June 2026
Semaglutide 2.4 mg Weekly injection. GLP-1 receptor agonist: weight loss and better metabolic control. US FDA, August 2025 (accelerated) · UK MHRA, July 2026 (conditional)

Metabolic drugs with liver benefit

  • GLP-1 agonists and tirzepatide: first choice when MASLD comes with type 2 diabetes or obesity.
  • SGLT2 inhibitors: protect heart and kidneys; safe in MASLD.
  • Pioglitazone: can help MASH in people with type 2 diabetes; watch weight gain and fluid retention.
  • Statins are safe in MASLD, including compensated cirrhosis. Do not stop them because of raised liver enzymes.

The pipeline to watch

  • FGF21 analogues (efruxifermin, pegozafermin): in phase 3 trials, including patients with cirrhosis.
  • GLP-1 combinations (with glucagon or GIP): in late-stage trials.
  • Outcome trials for the approved drugs are due from 2027, to show they prevent cirrhosis and liver events.

Bariatric and endoscopic options

Metabolic (bariatric) surgery

  • Consider it for people with MASLD and obesity who meet the usual surgery criteria.
  • It can resolve MASH and improve fibrosis in many patients.
  • Before surgery, stage the liver. Cirrhosis, and especially portal hypertension, raises surgical risk and needs a specialist centre.

Endoscopic options

  • Intragastric balloon and endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty lower weight with less invasion.
  • Liver data are promising but smaller than for surgery.
  • They can bridge to surgery or suit people who decline it.

Heart and metabolic risk

Most people with MASLD are more likely to die from heart disease than from liver disease. Every liver visit is also a heart visit.

Check

Blood pressure, HbA1c, full lipid profile, kidney function and urine albumin, weight and waist.

Look deeper

ApoB is more reliable than LDL when triglycerides are high. Consider measuring Lp(a) once in life.

Treat to target

Use statins and blood-pressure drugs as for anyone at the same heart risk. Choose diabetes drugs that also protect the heart.

Special groups

Group What is different
Lean MASLDNormal BMI does not rule it out. Assess fibrosis the same way. Aim for 3–5% weight loss and more activity.
MetALDMetabolic risk plus moderate alcohol (140–350 g a week in women, 210–420 g in men). Alcohol speeds damage, so ask about it carefully and advise cutting down or stopping.
Age 65 and overUse a FIB-4 cut-off of 2.0 instead of 1.3 to avoid false alarms.
PregnancyMASLD raises the risk of gestational diabetes and pre-eclampsia. Do not aim for weight loss or start MASH drugs during pregnancy; plan before conception.
Children and teenagersFIB-4 is not validated. Refer to paediatric gastroenterology and involve the whole family in lifestyle change.
