Medical disclaimer

Everything on this site is for general education only. It is not medical advice, and it is not a diagnosis or a treatment plan. It does not create a doctor–patient relationship.

Pages for doctors summarise published guidelines. They do not replace clinical judgement, the full guidelines, or the approved drug label in your country.

Do not stop, start or change any medicine because of this site. In an emergency, call your local emergency number or go to the nearest emergency department.

We work to keep the content accurate and current, but we cannot guarantee it. To the extent permitted by applicable law, the Metabolic Liver Academy and its contributors are not liable for loss or harm arising from reliance on this site.

Our tools

ToolForWhat it does, and its limits
Risk checklistPublicCounts common risk factors and suggests talking to a doctor. It does not estimate fibrosis and gives no diagnosis.
FIB-4 calculatorHealthcare professionalsEstimates the chance of advanced fibrosis in adults with MASLD. Not valid under 35, in children, in pregnancy or in acute liver injury. Full method and limits.
NAFLD fibrosis score (NFS)Healthcare professionalsEstimates advanced fibrosis from age, BMI, diabetes, AST, ALT, platelets and albumin (Angulo 2007). Less accurate in severe obesity and over 65, and not for children. Has a wide indeterminate range.
Agile 3+ and Agile 4Healthcare professionalsCombine FibroScan stiffness with routine bloods to estimate advanced fibrosis (Agile 3+) or cirrhosis (Agile 4) (Sanyal 2023). Only valid with a reliable FibroScan reading. Developed in adults in liver clinics.
FAST scoreHealthcare professionalsEstimates at-risk MASH (activity with fibrosis F2 or more) from stiffness, CAP and AST (Newsome 2020). Needs a reliable FibroScan reading. AST rises for many reasons unrelated to MASH.
FibroScan result explainerHealthcare professionalsPlaces a stiffness and CAP reading against published cut-offs (EASL–EASD–EASO 2024, Baveno VII). It does not read the scan itself. Food, hepatitis flares, heart failure and cholestasis can raise stiffness.
Fatty Liver Index (FLI) and Hepatic Steatosis Index (HSI)Healthcare professionalsEstimate whether fat is present in the liver (Bedogni 2006; Lee 2010). They do not measure fibrosis. HSI was developed in Asian adults, FLI in Italian adults.
HOMA-IRHealthcare professionalsA simple measure of insulin resistance from fasting glucose and insulin (Matthews 1985). Not valid in people taking insulin. Cut-offs differ between laboratories and populations.
BMI and waist-to-height ratioHealthcare professionalsStandard body measures (WHO; NICE NG246). They describe weight and belly fat, not liver health. BMI can mislead in very muscular people and in some ethnic groups.

Every tool calculates a published score. The formula, the cut-offs and the original paper are shown with each result, so the user can check the answer. Results are worded as a summary of published guidelines, not as a decision about a particular patient. Any tool can give a wrong result for some people. The tools are checked against the original papers with automated tests before each change.

Regulatory status. The tools are free educational aids and are not registered as medical devices. We are reviewing them against the Saudi Food and Drug Authority guidance on digital health products (MDS-G27). A tool will be changed or removed if that review requires it. A patient version of FIB-4 will only be added after this review.

Privacy

This notice explains what personal data the Academy and the services it uses receive, why, how long it is kept, and how you can ask for it to be deleted. We keep it to the minimum. The site has no accounts, no advertising and no cookies. Some services do receive limited data, as the table below shows.

Who is responsible

The data controller is Dr. Mahmoud Al Desoky, founder of the Metabolic Liver Academy, Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. Contact: info@metabolicliveracademy.org.

Why we use it

  • Mailing list: only with your consent, which you give by signing up and confirming by email. You can withdraw it at any time with the unsubscribe link.
  • Emails you send: to reply to you.
  • Visit counts and server logs: to keep the site working and secure, and to see which pages are useful. We never use any of this for advertising or sell it.

How long we keep it

  • Mailing list: until you unsubscribe. On request we delete your address completely.
  • Emails: kept only as long as needed to answer, and deleted within 12 months.
  • Visit counts: kept as overall statistics that do not identify you.
  • Server logs: kept by GitHub under its own policy. We cannot see or delete them.

Data processed outside Saudi Arabia

GitHub, GoatCounter and beehiiv run their services outside Saudi Arabia, mainly in the United States and the European Union. The data they receive is limited to what is listed above. By joining the mailing list, you agree that your email address is stored by beehiiv to send you the newsletter.

Your rights

You can ask what data we hold about you, ask us to correct or delete it, or withdraw consent. Email info@metabolicliveracademy.org. We aim to reply within 30 days. If you are not satisfied, you can contact the Saudi Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority (SDAIA).

Clinical governance: how content is made and checked

Who is responsible

  • Dr. Mahmoud Al Desoky, Founder and Director, is the editor and is responsible for the clinical accuracy of all content.
  • Invited reviewers will be specialists in the topic, chosen for expertise, with no relevant conflict of interest. Each will be named on the pages they review, with their disclosures.

How content is written

  • Based on current international guidelines (EASL, AASLD, AGA, AACE and others), the Saudi consensus, and peer-reviewed evidence. Every reference is checked against PubMed or the publisher.
  • When guidelines disagree, we say so and show each position. We do not write new recommendations.
  • Digital tools, including AI writing assistants, may help draft and check text. A doctor checks every clinical statement before it is marked as reviewed.
  • News is written in our own words with a link to the original. We do not copy articles.

Page records and versions

  • Each clinical page has a page record: author, version, last update, next review date, and its peer review status. "Checked by the author" means the founder has checked the page. "Independent peer review" is shown only after a named specialist has reviewed it, with their name and the date.
  • Every clinical statement is linked to a documented, published reference listed on the same page.
  • Pages are reviewed at least every 12 months, and sooner when a major guideline or drug approval changes.
  • Every change is kept in a public change history, so anyone can see what changed and when.

Corrections

Found an error? Email info@metabolicliveracademy.org. We aim to reply within 7 days and to fix confirmed clinical errors as a priority. Significant corrections are noted in the page's change history.

Conflicts of interest and sponsorship

  • The Academy currently has no sponsors and receives no funding.
  • Everyone who writes or reviews content (the founder included) discloses relevant financial relationships from the past 24 months: grants, speaker fees, advisory or consulting roles, trial roles, and shares. Disclosures are updated every 12 months and are published on the About page and beside reviewer names.
  • A person with a relevant conflict does not review content about that company's products.
  • If we accept support in future, each sponsor will be named on what it supports, and the support will be unrestricted. Sponsors never write, review, approve or see content before publication.
  • Patient pages describe medicines by type, not brand, and never promote a product.

  • Copyright concerns: if you believe something here uses your work without permission, email info@metabolicliveracademy.org with the page link and details. We will review it promptly and remove the material while we check, where appropriate.
